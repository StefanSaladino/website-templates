# Website Template Anti-AI Design Contract

Status: **LOCKED PROJECT-WIDE DESIGN CONTRACT**

These rules apply to every landing-page template in the Boomer Automation template library.

The goal is not to make every template minimal. The goal is to prevent the collection from looking like a set of generic AI-generated websites. Every template should feel intentionally art-directed, appropriate to its fictional business, and meaningfully different from the templates around it.

## 1. Fiction is allowed

These are demonstration websites. We may invent company names, products, services, customers, testimonials, prices, product screenshots, example dashboard data, user counts, performance statistics, addresses, team members, features, awards, dates and case studies.

**Fiction should support the concept, not manufacture visual sophistication.**

A fictional restaurant can show a fictional menu price. A fictional SaaS product can show believable sample project data. A fictional contractor can display a made-up customer testimonial. Avoid filling empty space with arbitrary numbers, charts, statuses, percentages or activity feeds simply because generated websites commonly contain them.

## 2. Every template needs a clear design idea

Before building a template, define its primary visual concept in one sentence. Examples: quiet editorial SaaS; brutalist creative studio; warm neighbourhood restaurant; photography-led architecture studio; conversion-first contractor; monochrome luxury fashion launch; typography-heavy conference page.

If the design cannot be described without words such as modern, sleek, premium, clean or cutting edge, the concept is not specific enough yet.

## 3. No card walls

Do not automatically turn every section into a grid of rounded cards. Avoid three identical feature cards because there are three features, six equal service boxes, KPI-card dashboards used as hero art, cards inside cards, testimonial-card grids by default, excessive rounded containers, and every piece of information receiving its own surface.

Prefer editorial rows, lists, split layouts, large image compositions, tables, timelines, horizontal bands, typographic sections, asymmetrical structures, overlapping sections and intentionally empty space. Cards are allowed when they are genuinely the right component; they are not the default layout primitive.

## 4. Avoid generic AI visual shorthand

Do not fall back on the generated-website starter kit: blue-purple aurora gradients, glowing orbs, glassmorphism everywhere, floating translucent cards, neon borders, giant blurred blobs, meaningless 3D objects, sparkles, random floating icons, excessive pill badges, gradient text on every headline, luminous dashboards, decorative tech grids, or huge rounded rectangles around whole sections.

These techniques are not individually forbidden. They are forbidden as shortcuts. If one is central to a deliberate art direction, use it sparingly.

## 5. Do not confuse complexity with quality

A strong landing page may consist of one headline, one image and one CTA. Another may contain ten sections. Neither is inherently better.

Do not add FAQs, testimonials, pricing, stats, logos, feature grids, comparison tables or newsletters merely because landing pages commonly contain them. Every section must have a reason to exist.

## 6. Heroes must vary

Do not reuse the same headline-left / image-right / two-buttons composition across the collection. Possible hero structures include centered typography, split screen, full-bleed photography, editorial offset, an image extending below the fold, diagonal composition, asymmetric grid, single-screen CTA, product interface as the main visual, vertical text, oversized typography, navigation integrated into the hero, layered photography, text over image, and unusual negative-space composition.

The collection should visibly demonstrate different approaches.

## 7. One primary CTA

Every page should have one clearly dominant conversion action. Secondary actions are allowed but must remain visually subordinate. Avoid three or four equally prominent buttons competing in the hero.

## 8. Fake data must still make sense

Sample content should behave like real content. Meaningful example release data, booking details, menu prices or project information are fine. Avoid fake precision or impressive-looking values that do not explain the fictional product or business.

## 9. Fake dashboards must represent a real workflow

A fictional SaaS template may contain a fictional dashboard, but it should answer: **What does this product actually help someone do?** Good examples include release planning, scheduling, invoices, bookings, inventory, campaign management, workouts or customer conversations.

Avoid dashboards composed of random charts, generic KPI cards, meaningless activity feeds, unrelated widgets and arbitrary health scores. A mock interface should make the fictional product easier to understand.

## 10. Charts require a reason

Illustrative data is permitted. Use a chart when showing growth, comparison, change over time, distribution or progress. Do not use charts simply because an empty rectangle needs filling. A table, sentence, number, list or image may communicate the idea better.

## 11. Typography must carry personality

Typography should help define the fictional brand. Vary serif vs sans-serif, condensed vs wide, uppercase vs sentence case, editorial vs geometric, oversized vs restrained, and loose vs tight rhythm.

Avoid excessive all-caps microcopy, giant letter spacing on every label, six levels of tiny grey metadata, or gradient text as a substitute for typography.

## 12. Copy should sound like the fictional business

Avoid generic AI marketing language such as “Unlock your potential,” “Elevate your experience,” “Empower your workflow,” “Revolutionize the way you work,” “Seamless solutions for modern teams,” and “Where innovation meets excellence.”

A contractor should sound different from a finance app. A restaurant should sound different from a developer tool. A fashion brand should sound different from a law firm.

## 13. Section headings should say something

Avoid repetitive structures such as “Our Features / Everything you need to succeed” or “Why Choose Us / Built differently for better results.” Prefer specific headings that advance the page's argument.

## 14. Use real visual hierarchy

Not every item deserves equal emphasis. One image may dominate a section. One feature may receive more space. Text may occupy one third of the screen. Large areas may remain empty. Sections may be intentionally unbalanced.

Asymmetry is encouraged when it improves composition. Do not invent content merely to make both sides equal.

## 15. Images need a purpose

Images should communicate product, atmosphere, place, craft, people, result or story. Avoid generic decorative imagery that could belong to any company. For photography-led templates, cropping and placement are part of the design rather than filler behind text.

## 16. Icons are optional

Do not put an icon beside every feature heading. Use icons when they improve recognition, communicate an action, support navigation, or represent a real concept efficiently.

## 17. Rounded corners are not a design system

Do not automatically apply large radii to every image, card, button, section, input and screenshot. Some templates may be square, slightly rounded, heavily rounded, circular, irregular or completely borderless.

## 18. Shadows should be deliberate

Avoid default floating-card shadows everywhere. Prefer borders, contrast, spacing, surface changes and layering before large box shadows. Strong shadows may fit a specific art direction, but should not appear automatically.

## 19. Colour palettes must belong to the concept

Do not make every template navy + cyan, black + electric purple, white + blue, or beige + black. Explore monochrome, earth tones, primary colours, muted editorial palettes, saturated campaign colours, warm neutrals, high-contrast black and white, and unusual brand-specific combinations.

Colour should differentiate concepts, not identify which templates were generated together.

## 20. Dark mode does not mean neon

A dark website can use charcoal, graphite, brown-black, ink, navy-black, warm black, muted colour, strong white typography, photography, texture, borders and typography. It does not automatically require glowing purple objects.

## 21. Tech does not mean dashboard

A technology landing page can be expressed through typography, code, documentation, workflow diagrams, product screenshots, diagrams, architecture, photography or editorial storytelling. Do not automatically build a KPI dashboard because the fictional company sells software.

## 22. Luxury does not mean beige serif

Luxury templates must not all become white background + serif headline + beige photography + huge whitespace. Luxury can also be dark, colourful, minimal, photographic, industrial, bold or culturally specific. Avoid turning stylistic categories into formulas.

## 23. Mobile gets its own composition

Mobile is not simply desktop stacked vertically. Check reading order, image crop, hero height, CTA placement, navigation, text scale, whitespace and touch targets. Desktop elements may need to disappear, reorder, simplify, crop differently or change alignment. There must be no horizontal overflow.

## 24. Navigation should fit the page

A one-screen waitlist page may need almost no navigation. A long service-business page may need several links. A product launch may need Product, Pricing and Sign in. Do not force the same header architecture onto every template.

## 25. Motion must earn its place

Animation may support section reveals, menu transitions, carousels, product state changes, scrolling relationships, meaningful hover feedback and image transitions. Avoid permanently floating objects, endless glowing pulses, arbitrary particle backgrounds and constant movement merely to make a page feel premium. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 26. Effects are allowed

The collection should experiment. Waves, curved transitions, diagonal cuts, clipping masks, parallax, scroll-driven effects, unusual image masks, horizontal scrolling, typography transformations, texture, grain, layered images, strong hover interactions, custom cursors and controlled distortion are allowed when conceptually justified.

**Do not use effects as a substitute for art direction.**

## 27. Templates must not look related by accident

When two adjacent templates are viewed in the gallery, they should not look like variations of the same design system. Vary spacing, navigation, typography, colour, hero structure, image treatment, border radius, section rhythm, CTA style and footer structure.

Shared technical quality is desirable. Shared visual DNA is not.

## 28. The thumbnail must represent the finished template

Once a template is complete, its gallery preview must be updated to reflect the actual page: colour, typography, hero layout, major image or product treatment and overall visual character. Do not leave concept artwork in the gallery once the real template exists.

## 29. Fictional brands should feel complete

Every template should have enough brand logic to feel intentional. Define brand name, industry, audience, primary action, tone and visual concept. We do not need a full branding exercise, but we need enough identity that the page does not read as Lorem Ipsum with a logo.

## 30. Do not over-explain the concept on the page

The visitor should experience the design. Do not add developer commentary such as “Built for conversion,” “Designed with modern UX principles,” “A clean and intuitive interface,” or “Strategic layout for maximum engagement.” That belongs in documentation, not the fictional website.

## 31. Accessibility is not optional

Every template must maintain semantic structure, readable contrast, keyboard access, visible focus states, meaningful alt text, correctly associated form labels, touch-friendly controls, reduced-motion support and logical heading order. Creative design does not override usability.

## 32. Responsive quality is part of the design

Every finished template should be checked around 320px, 375–430px, tablet, standard laptop and wide desktop. Look specifically for clipped headings, broken image crops, awkward line wrapping, huge empty mobile spaces, microscopic desktop-derived UI, navigation overflow and accidental horizontal scrolling.

## 33. No repeated component recipe

Do not repeatedly use Hero → logo strip → 3 feature cards → split image section → testimonial → pricing → FAQ → giant CTA. Individual templates may use those sections. The collection must not use them as a universal recipe.

## 34. Each template should demonstrate something

Every template should contribute at least one meaningful design idea to the collection: unusual hero composition, exceptional mobile navigation, wave transition, editorial typography, conversion-focused service structure, image masking, restrained one-page launch, asymmetric content flow, strong product demonstration, long-form storytelling or distinctive ecommerce presentation.

If a new template teaches nothing visually different from an existing one, redesign it.

## 35. Restraint is a feature

We do not need to fill every viewport. Empty space is allowed. Simple sections are allowed. Plain text is allowed. A page can be memorable because of what was intentionally omitted.

# Template Pre-Build Checklist

Before coding each template, define:

- **Template number**
- **Working name**
- **Industry**
- **Fictional brand**
- **Audience**
- **Primary CTA**
- **Length:** Short / Medium / Long
- **Visual concept**
- **Hero composition**
- **Primary typography direction**
- **Palette**
- **Distinctive element**
- **What makes it different from existing templates**

Only begin implementation when the final answer is clear.

# Final Quality Gate

Before marking a template complete, verify:

1. Does it look meaningfully different from the existing templates?
2. Is there one clear primary action?
3. Does every major section serve a purpose?
4. Are cards being used only where appropriate?
5. Are fictional numbers and data meaningful rather than decorative?
6. Does the copy fit the fictional business?
7. Is the typography intentional?
8. Does the mobile layout feel designed rather than collapsed?
9. Is animation purposeful?
10. Does the page avoid generic generated-site shorthand?
11. Does the gallery thumbnail accurately represent the finished page?
12. Would this look plausible if a real company shipped it?

If several answers are no, the template is not finished.
